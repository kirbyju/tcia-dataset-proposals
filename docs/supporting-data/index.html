01 / Prepare

Keep each record connected.

A clear table tells us what one row represents, what each value means and which subject or image it describes.

Use consistent identifiers

Use the same de-identified subject identifiers across tables and images. Identify examinations or visits separately. Keep filenames and relative paths exact and unique within your dataset.

Keep one observation per row

A subject can have several examinations, diagnoses or measurements. Give repeated observations their own records. Keep one variable per column and avoid merged cells or meaning conveyed only by color.

Document values and units

Keep units consistent and explain them in a dictionary. Distinguish unknown, not collected and not applicable values with your curator. A missing measurement is not zero.

Describe non-DICOM images

Preserve modality, anatomy and acquisition settings when available. Also document the submitted image’s geometry, intensity units and conversion history. A resampled image may have different spacing from its source.

Preserve detail that varies

Keep echo times, diffusion b-values, frames and channels distinguishable. Recommended tables use JSON arrays for repeated fields. Preserve the original pairing and ordering when those are needed to interpret the images.

Bring additional variables to your curator

Consult your data curator about how to provide additional data points you have collected. Supply patient-level information through the agreed TCIA curation workflow, using permitted de-identified identifiers and dates.

Acquisition and submitted-file properties can differ. Keep original settings when known and describe changes such as cropping, registration, resampling, normalization or compression.
